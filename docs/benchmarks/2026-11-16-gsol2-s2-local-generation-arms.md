# Local generation arms, 2026-11-16-gsol2, s2



The same Claude model wrote each repository's docs three ways: the openwiki skill (`openwiki`), the doc0 skill with the
map in `doc0_begin` (`doc0-map`), and the doc0 skill without it (`doc0-nomap`). Every arm got the same model, effort,
source SHA, prompt (only the skill's name differs), tool permissions, empty config folder, budget cap and time cap.
Generated by `pnpm bench:arms` from `corpora/arms/`; plan 4e in doc0-commercial.

Repositories: `sharkdp/fd@41532d114e2b`, `celery/celery@1432d9b6c686`, `All-Hands-AI/OpenHands@30604c40fc6e`. Host `codex`, model `gpt-6.1-sol`, effort `high`, credential `subscription`. Grounded rate judged by `deepseek-v4.1-flash`, every claim group (full population); blind comparison judged by `gemini-3.5-flash`.

## Before the full run: the two claim units differ

openwiki's claim unit is one proposition per Claim: its skill asks for "one coherent, independently falsifiable idea"
per Claim and puts completeness before a small count, and its Claims sit beside the page, adding no body words. doc0's
unit is one block's `Sources:` line, usually a whole paragraph. The same prose can make several openwiki claims and
one doc0 claim, so "grounded claims per 1,000 words" compares different units, and the grounded rate weighs a
paragraph against a sentence. The measures below are kept as the plan fixed them: the owner settles it before the full run.

## The decision rules, fixed before any run

This section is part of the report template and was written before any arm ran. It is never edited after a run.

Two measures, per arm and repository, both on the full claim population:
- **Grounded rate** = supported claim groups ÷ all claim groups. A group whose citations do not resolve (a missing file, a range past its end, openwiki evidence without a line range) counts as unsupported; it never leaves the denominator. The repository's `claimSupportRate` divides by resolved groups only, so a system that cites whole files would score on the few claims that resolve; it is shown for reference, never decided on. A group the judge failed on (an API error, after one retry) is the instrument's fault, not the arm's: it is left out of both numerator and denominator, and the report prints the count per cell.
- **Grounded claims per 1,000 words** = supported claim groups × 1000 ÷ the words of the arm's page bodies (`wordCount` over `stripCitations`'d bodies). A system cannot raise its grounded rate by making fewer claims without losing here.

The **resolution rate** (resolved groups ÷ all groups) is reported beside both, never decided on.

1. **The map stays in `doc0_begin`** if, over the repositories of the full run, doc0 with the map beats doc0 without it by at least 2 percentage points of mean grounded rate, or wins the blind comparison on both depth and breadth in at least two of three repositories; and in either case costs at most 25% more total tokens. Otherwise a follow-up removes the map from `begin` (the flag's default flips, then the code goes).
2. **The landing page may say doc0's generation beats openwiki** only if the better doc0 arm (by mean grounded rate) has a higher grounded rate **and** more grounded claims per 1,000 words than openwiki on every repository, and wins the blind comparison on a majority of dimension-repository cells. Otherwise the landing claims nothing comparative.
3. A run that hit its budget or time cap, crashed, or did not finish its lifecycle is reported as such and scored as nothing; it is never re-run selectively for one arm without re-running the others on that repository.

How the harness applies them:
- An arm is complete only when its finish tool's own result is in the transcript (`doc0_finish` answering `complete`, or
  `openwiki_finish` answering `complete` with no source change), and doc0's corpus holds exactly the pages that result lists.
- A claim group is a claim only when its text is prose: a line of a fenced block (code, a diagram), a table row or a
  diagram edge is not a claim, for every arm alike. doc0's skill puts a `Sources:` line after each code block, diagram
  and table, and the shared extractor would read the block's last line as the claim.
- Rates are compared unrounded, and rounded only to be shown. A pair within 2 points of grounded rate (rule 1's
  doc0-map and doc0-nomap, rule 2's better doc0 arm and openwiki, on any repository) is **undecided (second run needed)**:
  both arms run again there before the rule is applied (owner question 5).
- A cell with any judge failure is **undecided (re-judge)**, so a failure lowers neither measure. A measure with nothing
  to divide by (no claim group, no words) is shown as n/a and leaves the rule **undecided**, never 0%.
- "Two of three repositories" is read as ceil(2n/3) repositories when n repositories ran; total tokens are input, cache
  creation, cache read and output tokens summed over the repositories; a comparison cell is won only by a reliable
  `better` verdict (an unreliable cell is no win).
- A rule whose inputs are missing (an incomplete arm, or a report run without `--judged`) is **undecided** and changes
  nothing. On a dry run, or on fewer repositories than hono, fd and celery, the rules are **not applied**.
- The report refuses to compare arms made differently: every arm of a repository must share model, effort, credential,
  dry run, page cap, time cap, budget and source SHA, and all of them but the time cap and the SHA across repositories.

## Blind comparison

Pages are compared with their citations stripped (`Sources:` lines, inline citation links reduced to their label,
front matter, which holds openwiki's file-level sources), on depth, coherence and breadth only: citation quality is not
judged across systems. Each cell is three samples in both orders (`judgeComparative`); the first arm of a pair is "A".

Each cell: the verdict and median margin for A, then A's mean margin over the calls that showed the judge A first and over those that showed B first; the gap between the two is the judge's position bias. Every call is in the report's JSON.

## What this does not show

- One run per cell. Single runs vary; a pair within 2 points of grounded rate gets a second run of both arms before a
  rule is applied (owner question 5), and until then the rule reads undecided (second run needed).
- The judge is Gemini, one model family scoring another's pages. It reads each claim beside the cited lines only, so a
  true claim with a wrong citation scores unsupported, and a claim is only as checkable as its citation is precise.
- openwiki's claim population is its Claims sidecars; doc0's is the citations in its page bodies. Neither system's
  uncited prose is scored, which is why grounded claims per 1,000 words sits beside the rate.
- Three repositories, at the SHAs of their 2026-07 corpora, written by one Claude model at one effort. Nothing here says
  how either system does with another model, another host agent, or a repository unlike these.
